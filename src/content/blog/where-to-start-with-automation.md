Automation and AI are discussed everywhere, which can make it hard to know where to begin. In our experience, the best first step is not a big platform decision. It is finding one process that is clearly worth improving.

## Look for repetitive, rules-based work

Good candidates usually share a few traits:

- The same steps happen many times a week.
- The rules are mostly clear, even if there are exceptions.
- Information is copied from one place to another.
- Delays or mistakes cause real frustration.

Typical examples include routing incoming requests, extracting details from documents, sending reminders and preparing routine reports.

## Map the process before choosing tools

Write down how the work happens today, step by step, including who does what and where information lives. This often reveals that part of the problem is not technical at all — an unclear hand-off or a missing template — and it makes the automation far easier to design.

## Decide where people stay involved

Not every step should be automated. Decisions with financial, legal or customer impact often benefit from a person reviewing the outcome. A useful pattern is to let automation prepare the work — classify it, draft a response, fill in a form — and let a person approve it.

## Use AI where it adds something

Plain workflow automation can solve a surprising amount: moving data between systems, applying rules, sending notifications. AI becomes valuable when the input is unstructured, such as free-text emails or scanned documents. Using it only where it helps keeps systems simpler and easier to trust.

## Pilot, then extend

Choose one process, automate it carefully, and compare it with the old way of working. Once the first workflow is running reliably and the team trusts it, extending automation to related processes becomes much easier.
