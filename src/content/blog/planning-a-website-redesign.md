A website redesign often starts with a feeling: the site looks dated, or it no longer represents the business. That feeling is a good starting point, but it is not a brief. The most successful redesigns we see begin with a handful of clear decisions.

## 1. Who the site is really for

Most business websites serve several audiences — prospective customers, existing clients, partners, job seekers. List them, then decide which two or three matter most. Navigation, page order and calls to action all follow from this choice.

## 2. What visitors should be able to do

Write down the few actions that count as success: requesting a quote, booking a call, downloading a brochure, applying for a role. If an action is important, it deserves a clear, consistent place on the page — not a link buried in the footer.

## 3. What content you already have, and what is missing

A content inventory is unglamorous, but it prevents the most common delay in web projects: a finished design waiting for copy. Note which pages can be reused, which need rewriting and which do not exist yet.

## 4. Who will maintain the site

Be honest about who will update the website after launch and how often. This decides how content should be structured and which content management approach makes sense. A site that is easy for your team to edit stays accurate for longer.

## 5. What must not break

If your current site ranks in search, receives enquiries through forms or connects to other tools, list those dependencies. Redirects, tracking and integrations should be planned from the start so a new design does not quietly undo existing value.

## 6. How you will judge the result

Agree in advance how you will know the redesign worked. That might be the quality of enquiries, how easily people find key information, or how quickly the team can publish updates. Choosing the measure early keeps design discussions focused.
