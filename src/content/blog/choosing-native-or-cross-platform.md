One of the first questions in any mobile project is how the app should be built. The answer influences budget, timelines and how easy the app will be to maintain, so it is worth making deliberately.

## The three common options

**Native development** means building separate apps for iOS and Android using each platform's own languages and tools. It offers full access to device capabilities and fine control over performance.

**Cross-platform development** uses a single codebase — with frameworks such as React Native or Flutter — to produce apps for both platforms. Most of the code is shared, and platform-specific code can be added where needed.

**Progressive web apps** are websites that behave like apps: they can be installed on the home screen and work offline to a degree, but they have more limited access to device features and are not distributed in the same way as store apps.

## Questions that point to the right choice

- **Which device features do you rely on?** Heavy use of specialised hardware, background processing or advanced graphics can favour native development.
- **Do you need both platforms at launch?** If yes, a shared codebase usually reduces initial effort.
- **Who will maintain the app?** A single codebase can be simpler for a small team to look after over time.
- **Is store presence important?** If customers expect to find you in the App Store and Google Play, a web app alone may not be enough.

## Our usual starting point

For most business and customer-facing apps, we start by evaluating a cross-platform approach, because it lets one team deliver and maintain both platforms. We recommend native development when the requirements genuinely call for it — and we explain why.

## Decide with a prototype

Whatever the technology, an early prototype on real devices is the best way to test assumptions. It reveals usability issues and technical risks while they are still cheap to fix.
